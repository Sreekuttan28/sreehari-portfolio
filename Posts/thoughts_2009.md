The Cost of Empathy

Over the last five years, the learning curve has been relentless. Every day brings new challenges, and while I’m proud of the impact I’m making, it hasn't come without a quiet, underlying struggle. The daily grind often begins with friction, but I've finally started to accept the weight of it as part of the process.

One of the hardest lessons I've had to learn came from trusting people blindly. When you extend your trust and it leads to emotional burnout and mental blocks, the hurt runs deep. It makes you pause and wonder: If I had known how this would end, would I have avoided making the mistake?

Honestly? I don't think so. Most of the time, we don't act with calculated foresight; we act spontaneously in the spur of the moment. We see someone who needs a hand, and our instinct is to reach out. Sometimes, that well-intentioned help backfires and becomes the very thing that pulls you down. 

But here is the takeaway: never let a bad experience harden your heart. The fact that your kindness was mishandled is a reflection of the situation, not a flaw in your character. Keep lending that hand. The world needs people who are brave enough to help, even when they know the risks. True growth isn't about avoiding mistakes—it's about surviving them with your empathy intact.
